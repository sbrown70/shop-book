Radio, amp & tach wiring

01System at a glance

One fused feed runs from the battery side through the firewall to the fuse block under the dash. The key only clicks the block's relay, and every accessory has its own fuse in the block.

04Making connections

Every connection is a crimp with a ratcheting crimper, sealed with adhesive heat-shrink, and every one must pass a hard tug test. Terminal color matches wire size: red 22–18 ga, blue 16–14 ga, yellow 12–10 ga.

Never

  • use snap-on "vampire" taps (Scotchlok, T-taps), twist-and-tape splices or wire nuts.
  • pass a wire through a metal hole without a grommet.
  • leave a power wire unfused, or put the fuse at the wrong end of the wire.
  • sandwich ring terminals under a battery clamp bolt.

05Install steps

Work in order. Where the Painless instruction sheet in the box differs from this one, follow Painless.

1

Find the ACC source

battery
connected
  • Meter on DC volts, black probe on bare metal. Find a wire under the dash that reads battery voltage in ACC and RUN and 0 V in OFF. The original radio feed is usually it.
  • Check it at every key position, then mark it with tape. It's normal for it to drop out while cranking. Don't trust 60-year-old wire colors.
  • Not the coil feed: in RUN it runs through a resistor wire at reduced voltage.
2

Disconnect and repair

  • Disconnect the battery negative cable and tuck it away from the post.
  • Inspect every factory wire you tapped into in high school. Cut out nicked, burnt or brittle sections and butt-splice in new wire of the same gauge.
  • Remove leftover connectors, tape and orphaned wire.
  • Plug unused holes from the old firewall runs with rubber grommet plugs.
  • Clean and tighten the battery-to-engine and engine-to-body grounds. Add an engine-to-firewall strap if there isn't one.
3

Mount the fuse block

  • Pick a spot under the dash where you can reach the fuses, clear of the column, pedals, heater box and any coolant drip. Near the passenger kick panel usually works.
  • Mount it with the kit's long screws and spacers, to the dash support or a small plate, never to anything that moves.
  • Sand a bare-metal ground point nearby and connect the kit's ground wire to it. The radio and tach will ground here too.
4

Run the main feed

  • Choose the firewall pass-through: an unused factory grommet, or a new hole in a flat spot after checking both sides for brake lines, the heater box and the factory harness. Deburr it and fit a grommet.
  • Route the kit's 10 ga red wire from the block through the grommet toward the battery.
  • Mount the kit's circuit breaker close to the battery, on the inner fender or firewall. Cut the red wire to length and connect it to the breaker.
  • Run the leftover red from the breaker's other terminal to the starter relay battery stud (or a battery terminal with auxiliary studs). Crimp the ring, but leave it unbolted until testing.
5

Connect the trigger

  • Route the kit's pink 18 ga wire to the ACC wire you marked in step 1.
  • Splice it in as shown in section 04. Painless's sheet also describes clipping a fuse tap onto the hot side of a fuse that's live only with the key on. That works too if your fuse panel has such a fuse and the tap fits it.
6

Assign and label circuits

  • Pick which pre-attached circuit wire feeds each accessory and write the slot numbers in the circuit plan.
  • Label both ends of every wire now, before anything is buried behind the dash.
7

Radio

  • Connect to the radio's own harness with butt connectors, never to the radio itself.
  • Yellow to its constant circuit, red to the 5 A ignition circuit, black to the ground point.
  • Orange (if present) to the dash light circuit, the instrument-light feed that changes with the headlight-switch dimmer.
  • Leave blue/white long enough to reach the amp turn-on run (step 9). Check the radio's manual for its exact colors and fuse values.
8

Tachometer

  • Power lead to the 3 A ignition circuit, ground to the ground point, light lead to the dash light circuit.
  • Run the signal wire through a firewall grommet to the coil − terminal, ring terminal stacked under the existing nut.
  • Keep it away from the spark plug wires, which make the needle jumpy. Set the tach's cylinder selector to 8.
  • Coil terminals are shown in section 02.
9

Amplifier

  • Splice the 15 A constant circuit's wire to the 12 ga red. If the gauges differ, use a butt connector sized for the bigger wire and fold the smaller wire's strands double so it fills the barrel.
  • Run the red down one side under the door sill plates and carpet to the trunk. It stays inside the car, so no extra firewall hole.
  • Run the RCA cables and the turn-on wire down the center tunnel or the other side.
  • Mount the amp on a board or bracket where it gets air, not loose on the trunk floor or under carpet.
  • Ground it with under 3 ft of 12 ga black to sanded bare metal on the trunk floor or a body brace. Seal any hole you drill.
  • Yellow ring or fork terminals on power and ground, red on the turn-on wire, into the amp's screw strip. Turn-on goes to the radio's blue/white lead.
  • Leave the amp's own fuse in. The block fuse protects the wire; the amp's fuse protects the amp.
10

Route and secure

  • Sleeve every engine-bay run and keep it several inches from the exhaust manifolds, and clear of the fan, belts, throttle linkage and steering shaft.
  • Under the dash, bundle wires and keep them clear of the pedals, steering column and parking brake.
  • Tie or clamp every 12–18 in, with a little slack at each end.
  • Add a small drip loop just before each firewall grommet.
  • Fold this sheet with the filled-in circuit plan into the glovebox.

06Testing

Power up in stages so a mistake blows a fuse instead of melting a wire.

A

Before the battery

  • Pull every fuse from the block. The breaker's battery-side ring is still unbolted.
  • Meter on resistance: breaker's block-side terminal to bare metal. Reads open (OL)?  A reading near 0 Ω is a short; find it before going on.
B

First power-up

  • Bolt the breaker's ring to its stud and reconnect the battery negative. Block fuses still out.
  • Constant circuits at the fuse slots, key off (expect about 12.6 V): V
  • Ignition circuits at the fuse slots: OFF V   ACC V   RUN V, with a relay click
  • Radio fuses in: radio turns on with the key and keeps a preset after key off and back on.
  • Tach fuse in: lights with the dash lights and reads a sensible idle. Idle reading: rpm
  • Amp's 15 A fuse in: amp turns on with the radio and off with the key.
  • Set the amp gain per its manual with the radio at about three-quarters volume.
C

Voltage drop

engine running,
amp playing loud
  • Battery + to amp +12 V: V   (under about 0.75 V; this path runs through the fuse block, so a little more drop is normal)
  • Amp ground to battery −: V   (under about 0.5 V)

More than that points to a bad crimp, a loose connection or a poor ground.

D

Key-off drain

  • Everything off and the doors shut so no courtesy lights are on. Meter in series with the battery negative cable, starting on the 10 A range: mA
  • An original electric dash clock, if you have one, rewinds every few minutes and briefly spikes the reading. That's normal.
  • Radio memory adds a few mA up to about 30 mA. Above about 50 mA, pull fuses one at a time until the reading drops.
  • Don't crank the engine or switch on the headlights with the meter in line.
E

After a week of driving

  • Recheck every nut and ground bolt.
  • After a long drive, feel each connection. One that's noticeably warm has high resistance; redo it.
