> **Note added during import:** this file comes from an earlier chat that assumed a 289 with a points distributor. The engine is actually a 1968 Ford 302 (from a Fairlane), with an electronic ignition conversion. The engine has since been pulled and torn down; treat this as background.

# Pulling the engine from a 1966 Mustang (C4 Automatic Stays in the Car)

**The plan:** Separate the engine from the transmission at the bellhousing and lift the engine out the top. The C4, torque converter, and everything behind the bellhousing stay in the car on the rear mount (supported). Reserve a full weekend for the pull alone; don't rush the converter/flexplate separation.

---

## Tools & Supplies to Have on Hand

- Engine hoist (2-ton), plus a load leveler/tilter if you can get one — makes clearing the core support far easier
- Lift plate or Grade 8 chain + bolts to bolt to the intake, OR chain around the manifolds
- Floor jack + jack stand + a scrap 2x4 (to support the trans once the engine is gone)
- Torque converter retaining strap or a scrap bracket + bolts
- Drain pans (coolant, oil), sealable jugs
- Sockets/ratchets (3/8" and 1/2" drive), long extensions, swivel/universal joint
- Line wrenches (fuel, cooler lines), penetrating oil
- Breaker bar + socket for the crank pulley bolt (to rotate the engine by hand)
- Engine stand (min. 750 lb) with the correct small-block Ford mount bolts
- Zip-top bags + Sharpie for labeling hardware, phone for reference photos
- Masking tape + pen for labeling wires and hoses

> Label and bag hardware by area (radiator, exhaust, accessories, bellhousing). Photograph every connector, bracket, and hose routing before you touch it. Future-you will be grateful.

---

## Stage 1 — Prep & Drain

- [ ] Park on level ground, wheels chocked, transmission in Park.
- [ ] Disconnect the **negative** battery cable first, then positive. Remove the battery from the tray if it's in your way.
- [ ] Drain the cooling system: open the radiator petcock (or pull the lower hose) into a pan. Ford blocks also have a drain plug on each side — optional but thorough.
- [ ] Drain the engine oil (you'll be tipping the engine; less mess). Leave the filter on for now.
- [ ] Relieve/plug the fuel line so it doesn't siphon when disconnected.

## Stage 2 — Hood & Top-Side Access

- [ ] Mark the hood hinge positions with a paint pen or scribe so it goes back in the same spot.
- [ ] With a helper holding each side, unbolt the hood from the hinges (4 bolts) and set it aside on a blanket, on edge against a wall.
- [ ] Remove the air cleaner assembly.

## Stage 3 — Cooling System

- [ ] Remove upper and lower radiator hoses.
- [ ] Disconnect the two **transmission cooler lines at the radiator** (lower tank). Use a line wrench. These lines stay attached to the C4 — just free them from the radiator. Plug/cap them and be ready for a little ATF.
- [ ] Remove the fan shroud (if fitted).
- [ ] Unbolt and remove the radiator (typically 4 bolts to the core support). Lift it straight up and set it somewhere safe — it's easy to bend fins.
- [ ] Remove the fan, spacer, and pulley from the water pump (4 bolts). Keep the fan bolts together.

## Stage 4 — Fuel, Throttle & Vacuum

- [ ] Disconnect the fuel line at the fuel pump or carb inlet. Cap it.
- [ ] Disconnect the throttle linkage/rod at the carburetor.
- [ ] Disconnect the **kickdown (TV) linkage** where it meets the carb. (The rest of the kickdown linkage stays with the trans.)
- [ ] Disconnect the choke cable/wire if fitted.
- [ ] Pull all vacuum lines off the carb/intake (mark them) — brake booster, PCV, distributor advance, etc.

## Stage 5 — Electrical (label every wire)

- [ ] Coil wires (+ and –) and the wire to the distributor.
- [ ] Alternator wiring (charge lead + field plug) and remove the alternator, or unbolt and hang it aside.
- [ ] Starter cable at the starter.
- [ ] Temperature sender wire, oil pressure sender wire.
- [ ] Any engine-to-body ground strap (usually block/head to firewall).
- [ ] Wire to the neutral safety switch / backup switch stays with the trans — just make sure nothing tethers the engine to the harness.

## Stage 6 — Accessories

- [ ] **Power steering (if equipped):** unbolt the pump and set it aside with its hoses still attached if they'll reach; otherwise disconnect and cap the two lines. Don't lose the drive belt spacers.
- [ ] **A/C (if equipped — rare on these):** do **not** open the refrigerant system. Unbolt the compressor and hang it aside with hoses attached and charged.
- [ ] Remove the drive belt(s).
- [ ] Disconnect both **heater hoses** at the engine (water pump and intake/head fittings).

## Stage 7 — Exhaust

- [ ] Penetrating oil on the manifold-to-pipe bolts/nuts ahead of time.
- [ ] Disconnect each exhaust pipe at the manifold flange (2 studs/bolts per side). If they're seized and you're not reusing the manifolds, be prepared to fight them or cut.
- [ ] The manifolds can come out with the engine — no need to remove them from the heads.

## Stage 8 — Motor Mounts

- [ ] Remove the through-bolt/nut on each engine mount (one per side) that ties the engine bracket to the frame stand. Penetrating oil helps; a swivel and long extension reaches the passenger side.
- [ ] Leave the mount brackets on the block.

---

## Stage 9 — Separate the Engine from the C4 (the important part)

- [ ] **Remove the starter** (2 bolts). It sits right where you need access and stays with the car.
- [ ] Remove the lower **bellhousing inspection cover / dust shield** at the bottom of the bellhousing. Now you can see the flexplate and the torque converter.
- [ ] **Unbolt the torque converter from the flexplate.** There are **4 nuts (or bolts)**. Put a socket on the crank pulley bolt with a breaker bar and rotate the engine by hand to bring each one into view through the opening. Remove all 4. **The converter stays with the transmission — never let it hang on the flexplate.**
- [ ] Confirm the flexplate is now free of the converter (nothing but the crank holding it).
- [ ] Remove the **bellhousing-to-block bolts** — about **6** around the perimeter of the bellhousing. The top ones are the awkward ones; a long extension with a swivel from above, feeling down the back of the block, gets them. Double-check you've found them all before pulling — a hidden top bolt will stop the engine from separating and tempt you to force it (don't).
- [ ] Leave connected to the trans (they all stay): shift linkage, kickdown linkage, dipstick tube, cooler lines, neutral safety switch wiring, and the rear/trans mount.

## Stage 10 — Support the Transmission

- [ ] Put the floor jack (or a jack stand) with a 2x4 pad under the C4 pan — spread the load, don't press on the thin stamped pan directly.
- [ ] Take up the weight so that when the engine leaves, the front of the trans **won't drop** and strain the rear mount and tailshaft. It should sit roughly level.

## Stage 11 — Rig the Hoist

- [ ] Bolt a lift plate to the intake (carb removed or worked around), **or** run Grade 8 chain to two solid points — a bolt into an intake boss at the front and a manifold/head bolt at the rear, arranged so the engine hangs slightly **nose-up**.
- [ ] Snug the hoist until it just takes the engine's weight. Recheck that every wire, hose, line, and linkage is disconnected. Walk around the whole engine once.

## Stage 12 — Lift & Separate

- [ ] Raise slightly to unload the mounts. Confirm the engine is free and level-ish.
- [ ] **Pull the engine straight FORWARD first** to slide the flexplate off the converter snout. Don't lift while the flexplate is still engaged — you'll drag on the converter and can bend the input shaft/pump. A load leveler makes this pull-and-tilt controllable.
- [ ] Once it's clear of the converter, tilt **nose-up** and lift, easing it up and forward to clear the core support. Go slow; watch the firewall and the trans.
- [ ] The moment the engine is off the converter, have your helper **secure the converter** so it can't slide forward off the front pump (strap it, or bolt a bracket across the bellhousing face). If it slides forward it dumps ATF and unseats from the pump — a pain to re-seat.
- [ ] Lift clear, swing away, and lower onto the engine stand. Bolt up to the stand before unhooking the hoist.

---

## After It's Out — Don't Forget

- **Converter stays put and full.** Leave it strapped and seated on the trans. On reinstall, verify it's fully home (you'll feel it drop in over the pump with two or three distinct engagements) before mating the engine, or you'll crack the front pump.
- **Cover the open bellhousing** with a rag/bag to keep dirt out of the trans.
- **Trans stays supported** the whole time the engine is out.
- While access is this good, knock out the rear-of-block jobs: the **rear main seal** (your reason for being here), the **cam/welch plug** behind the flexplate, **rear core plugs**, and the **oil pan gasket**. Inspect the chipped seal register now while you can see and clean it.

## Reinstall Preview (so you pull it correctly)

- Engine goes back **nose-up**, flexplate lined up to the converter studs, mated to the bellhousing **before** you run the converter bolts.
- Get the bellhousing bolts snug and the engine seated flush to the trans **first**, then pull the converter forward to the flexplate and install the 4 nuts/bolts. Never use those converter bolts to draw the converter forward — that's how pumps crack.
- Torque references: bellhousing-to-block bolts ~40–45 ft-lb; converter-to-flexplate ~20–25 ft-lb; motor mount through-bolts ~45–55 ft-lb. Verify against a 1966 Mustang/289 shop manual for your exact hardware.
