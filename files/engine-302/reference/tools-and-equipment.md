# Tools & Equipment — Reference & Decisions

## Engine hoist: buy vs. rent (decided: buy or AutoZone loaner)

**The job's shape is what decides it.** You need the hoist to *pull* the engine, then it sits unused through all the bench work (inspect the chip, clean the bore, do the seal, cam plug, pan gasket), then you need it again to *reinstall*. That middle stretch can run one to several weeks at a hobbyist pace with parts on order. A daily/weekly rental meters the whole time whether you're using it or not.

**Options:**
- **Buy — Harbor Freight 2-ton foldable shop crane (~$270).** Folds flat against a wall when done, so storage isn't an issue. Reviews report years of hard use. You own it for the pull *and* the reinstall with no clock running, and it resells well. *Recommended default.*
- **AutoZone Loan-A-Tool (the wildcard).** It's a **refundable deposit**, not a rental fee — keep the tool up to 90 days, get the deposit back on return. 90 days covers the whole job for effectively free. Catches: engine hoists aren't stocked at every store and loaner units can be beat-up. **Call local stores first to confirm one's in stock.**
- **Daily/weekly rental (e.g., Sunbelt ~$43/day, ~$155/week).** *Skip for this job* — two or three weeks of rental meets or beats the purchase price and you own nothing.

**Bottom line:** If a nearby AutoZone has a loaner, borrow it free. Otherwise buy the HF foldable. Don't do daily/weekly rental here.

*Optional add-on:* a load leveler / engine tilter makes clearing the core support and sliding the flexplate off the converter much more controllable. Worth it if you can get one.

## Transmission support: no trans jack needed

Because the C4 **stays in the car**, you're not lowering it out, so a tall tilting transmission jack is overkill. You just need to hold the front of the trans up after the engine leaves so it doesn't hang off the rear crossmember mount and strain the tailshaft.

Use: **floor jack (or jack stand) + a scrap 2x4** across the pan to spread the load — never jack directly on the thin stamped pan or you'll dent it and cause leaks. A jack stand is more stable than a jack for leaving it parked while you work.

Only rent a proper trans jack if the plan later changes to actually dropping the C4 out.

## Torque converter retaining strap

Once the engine is off, the converter wants to slide forward and unseat from the front pump — which dumps ATF and makes reinstall a pain (and can crack the pump). Keep it pushed back and strapped: a converter retaining strap, or a scrap bracket bolted across the bellhousing face.

## Tool & supply checklist

- Engine hoist (2-ton) + optional load leveler
- Lift plate or Grade 8 chain + bolts for the intake
- Floor jack + jack stand + scrap 2x4
- Torque converter retaining strap (or bracket + bolts)
- Drain pans + sealable jugs (coolant, oil, ATF)
- Socket/ratchet sets (3/8" & 1/2" drive), long extensions, swivel/U-joint
- Line wrenches (fuel & cooler lines), penetrating oil
- Breaker bar + socket for the crank bolt (to rotate engine by hand)
- Engine stand (750 lb min) with small-block Ford mount bolts
- Zip-top bags + Sharpie, masking tape + pen (label hardware, wires, hoses)
- Phone for reference photos before disconnecting anything
