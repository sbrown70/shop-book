# Rear Main Seal — Reference Notes

## The core job

The 289 uses a **two-piece rear main oil seal**: a neoprene lip seal split at the crank centerline. The **upper half sits in a groove machined into the block**; the **lower half sits in the rear main bearing cap**. Normally this is a job you can do with the engine in the car and the crank still in place — you drop the pan, pull the rear cap to change the lower half, and rotate the upper half out around the crank with a seal tool or brass punch. We're doing it engine-out because of the block damage and the fight the old seal put up.

## Why this one escalated

- The old seal would not come out — almost certainly because whoever did it last **smeared RTV all over it**. The correct technique is sealant on the **seal ends only**, not packed around the whole seal.
- Getting the old seal out of the rear main cap required scraping all the rubber off with a pick and then pulling it. That same gunk is likely packed into the **upper (block) half of the bore** too, and it all has to come out or the new seal won't seat.
- While trying to punch the old seal out, a **chip broke off the very rear of the block, transmission side.**

## The chip — how to judge it

The thing that matters is whether the chip breaks into the **semicircular groove where the seal's outer diameter seats against the block**:

- **Rear edge / chamfer only** → not a sealing surface. Fine to proceed. The very rear lip doesn't hold pressure. (This is the most likely case based on where it is.)
- **Part of the groove wall gone** → oil can weep past the seal OD at that spot. Still fixable without machining:
  - A dab of anaerobic sealant or RTV on the seal's **outer diameter** at that spot on install, or
  - Fill the void with epoxy / JB Weld — it's a low-stress, non-bearing area.

Inspect and clean it under good light with the engine out before deciding. You do **not** need to pull the crank to judge or fix this — the accessible portion plus a mirror-and-feel check is enough for a rear-edge chip.

## Do you need to pull the crank?

No. The crank blocks the top of the upper seal bore, so seeing the *entire* upper groove with your eyes would require removing the crank — but that jumps the scope into main-bearing/rebuild territory (clearances, Plastigage, cap torque sequence). For this repair:
- **Lower half (cap):** 100% visible the moment the cap is off.
- **Upper half (block):** visible at the parting lines and partway up each side; the top is behind the crank — use a mirror, light, and fingertip. Plenty to clean the bore, judge a rear-edge chip, and seat the new seal.
- Only pull the crank if the chip turns out to have taken a real bite out of the upper groove wall **and** you can't clean or judge it by mirror-and-feel.

## Installing the new seal — key points

- **Clean the bore completely** of old RTV first. This is the #1 cause of a repeat leak here.
- Sealant on the **seal ends only**; lube the **lip** (engine oil or assembly lube).
- **Offset the seal parting lines from the cap-to-block split** so the two gaps don't line up (a common leak path).
- Re-torque the main cap to spec and check that the crank still turns freely.

## Don't skip: inspect the crank seal surface

A grooved or scored crank journal where the lip rides will cause the **new** seal to leak too. Check it while the engine's out. If it's worn, plan a thin repair sleeve (e.g., Speedi-Sleeve) rather than just hoping a new seal fixes it.

## While you're in there

Access to the rear of the block is as good as it will ever get. Knock out the common leak sources now:
- Cam / welch plug behind the flexplate
- Rear core (freeze) plugs
- Oil pan gasket
